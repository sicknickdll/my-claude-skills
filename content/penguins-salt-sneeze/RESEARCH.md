# Penguins drink seawater (and sneeze out the salt)

## Hooks (ranked)
1. **"Did you know? Penguins can drink seawater."** — surprising, simple, sets up a "how?".
2. "Penguins sneeze out salt." — funnier but gives the answer away.
3. "Why don't penguins get thirsty at sea?"

## Core explanation (beats)
1. Penguins can drink seawater; for humans it causes dehydration.
2. Above each eye they have a salt gland (supraorbital gland).
3. It pulls excess salt (sodium chloride) out of the blood — far more efficiently than kidneys.
4. The salty liquid drains out through the nostrils on the beak; they drip it off or sneeze/shake it out.

## Simplifications / pitfalls
- "Filter" is a simplification: the gland actively secretes salt, it's not a sieve.
- Penguins mostly get water from food and seawater; they also eat snow/drink fresh water when available.
- Glands sit in a groove on the skull above the eye socket, not visibly "on" the eye.
